What this is — and what it is not

Each tick, every channel draws a fresh 200-bit trial and is scored against pure chance (a Binomial(200, ½): mean 100, SD ≈ 7.07). The two quantities the Global Consciousness Project tracked are shown live: the network mean (Stouffer Z across channels) and, more to your point, the inter-channel variance — how much the generators disagree with each other. Under independence that variance sits at 1.000. A drop below it means the channels are moving together: order leaking into noise. The chart accumulates the network-variance deviation against a chance envelope; sustained excursions are quantified as a coherence Z and odds-against-chance.

The honest caveat. The entropy here is the browser's cryptographic RNG — an algorithm seeded by the operating system, not a physical/quantum random-event generator. By construction it stays random; it has no physical noise source for a "field of attention" to perturb, and any micro-bias would be erased by the OS whitening anyway. So this instrument cannot, in principle, reproduce the GCP's claimed effect. The detector is real and correct; the source is not a physical REG.

The Test correlation (ρ) slider injects an artificial shared fluctuation into every channel so you can watch what a genuine anomaly would look like — the variance collapsing, the chart breaking its envelope, the odds climbing. It is a calibration signal, not consciousness. Set it to zero to see true independence.

To attempt the real experiment you need raw output from hardware quantum/thermal REGs read unconditioned (the PEAR-lineage devices the GCP used), or a verifiable physical feed such as NIST/CU Boulder's CURBy beacon — piped into exactly this statistical pipeline.
