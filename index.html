METHODOLOGY & HONEST CAVEATS ›

What it samples

Real-time total interplanetary magnetic field |B| (Bt) measured at the L1 Lagrange point and published by NOAA/NWS Space Weather Prediction Center at roughly 1-minute cadence. The whole Sun → solar-wind → magnetosphere system is the physical generator; the spacecraft is the sampler.

Baseline & residual

A trailing 31-minute moving average is removed: residual = Bt − baseline. This strips the slow, known geophysical drift and leaves the fast fluctuation, which is what we test. Note: literal 27-day solar-rotation recurrence (comparing now against one rotation ago) is the stronger control but needs the SWPC archive endpoint; this self-contained build uses an in-feed rolling detrend, swappable for true recurrence when hosted server-side.

The null is frozen, not fitted live

Residual mean μ and SD σ are computed once from a reference window (the first ~70% of the first load) and then frozen. Every incoming residual is standardized z = (r − μ) / σ against that fixed reference, so the statistic can genuinely move if the live field departs from baseline.

The statistic

Test samples are thinned to 1 per 5 min to limit autocorrelation, then combined: Z = Σz / √N, which is N(0,1) under the null. Deviation is |Z| in σ; two-tailed p = 2(1 − Φ(|Z|)); the 95% CI on the per-sample mean shift is δ̂ ± 1.96/√N with δ̂ = Z/√N. If the CI brackets 0, nothing is detected — which is the honest, expected result.

Why this stays honest

IMF fluctuations are autocorrelated and non-stationary, so the nominal √N envelope is approximate — real excursions almost always mean space weather (shocks, storms, diurnal structure), not consciousness. And if you run any null long enough you will cross p<0.05 about 1 session in 20 by pure chance. That is calibration, not discovery. The field-state pill states the calibrated odds rather than claiming a "signal," and nothing here is user-adjustable.
